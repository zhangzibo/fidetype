\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We defined a minimal per-frame output format for neural text-entry decoders and a trust layer that reads only this format and decides whether to commit, offer or abstain under a stated risk bound. Re-fitted per device, the same implementation turned frequent wrong commits into rare ones on one intracortical participant at the speed of always committing and slightly faster than always offering, while one committed sentence in four was still wrong. On sEMG sentences composed from isolated presses it almost never committed, and on a weak MEG decoder it correctly never committed. Conformal risk control met its target on exchangeable splits, and online re-calibration kept the long-run rate near the target when the error level of the test data differed from the calibration data. The layer committed no text from signal-destroying streams, but it cannot detect a real stream of a different intended sentence, and it bounds the marginal risk, not the error among commits. All speed results come from a simulated user. The next steps are a study with gaze-typing users, which needs ethics approval; a controller for the error among commits; and a public benchmark built on the same harness and controls.
