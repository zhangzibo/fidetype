\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A trust layer that reads only a per-frame output stream can decide whether a neural text-entry decoder commits, offers or abstains, with a high-probability bound on the wrong-commit rate. On one intracortical participant, Learn-then-Test with the max rule kept wrong commits at or below 5\% in 92--98\% of repeated deployments at little speed cost relative to always offering, while about one in five (our decoder) or one in thirteen (published decoder) committed sentences was still wrong; conformal risk control and the plug-in rule met the target only on average. A guaranteed error among commits of 10\% was reachable only for the strongest decoder, and on composed sEMG sentences and a weak MEG decoder the layer almost never or never committed. Calibration data must play no other role. Next steps are a confirmatory test of the frozen configuration on new data, a user study that measures which error rates users accept, and the public benchmark that the release starts.
