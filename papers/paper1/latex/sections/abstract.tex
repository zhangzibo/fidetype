\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textit{Objective.} Neural text-entry decoders increasingly end in a language model that turns weak neural evidence into fluent sentences, but they rarely signal when a sentence is wrong. We ask when such a system should commit a sentence without confirmation, and how to bound how often it commits wrong text.
\textit{Approach.} We define an open per-frame output format (\nts{}) and a trust layer that reads only this stream, calibrates confidence and chooses to commit, offer alternatives or abstain, with the commit threshold set by conformal risk control, Learn-then-Test or adaptive online updates. We re-fit one implementation offline on public data from one intracortical speech participant (816 test trials), forearm EMG key presses composed into sentences (19 participants) and MEG typing (19 participants). The primary outcome is the wrong-commit rate per sentence attempted, with the commit rate and the error rate among commits; a simulated gaze-typing user gives effective words per minute (\ewpm{}).
\textit{Main results.} On intracortical speech, the layer cut wrong commits from 61\% to 5.9\% of sentences (our decoder) and from 19\% to 4.4\% (published decoder); 25\% and 9\% of committed sentences were still wrong. It was 0.18 and 0.66 \ewpm{} faster than always offering (95\% CIs over days \ci{0.12}{0.24}, \ci{0.53}{0.80}), but under most user-model variants not faster than always committing. On EMG and MEG it committed at most 4\% and 0\% of sentences, and gaze typing with word completion was faster on MEG and the two weaker EMG decoders. With calibration data disjoint from all fitted parts, offline conformal calibration gave 2.5--6.0\% on later data (target 5\%), never significantly above the rate for exchangeable data; an apparent drift in our earlier analysis came from reusing fit data. The layer committed no text from signal-destroying control streams but cannot detect a real stream of a different intended sentence.
\textit{Significance.} A stream-level commit rule makes the commit decision explicit and bounds how often wrong text is sent, at little or no speed cost relative to always offering. Speed gains are secondary, user-model dependent and need a user study.
\end{abstract}
