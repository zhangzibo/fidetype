\begin{abstract}
Neural text-entry decoders, from non-invasive magnetoencephalography (MEG) to intracortical arrays and wearable electromyography (EMG), now produce fluent text. They rarely signal when that text is wrong, and each device reports confidence in its own format. We present Fidetype, an open, vendor-neutral stream format for per-frame decoder output (\nts{}) and a decoder-agnostic trust layer that reads only this stream. The layer calibrates confidence; chooses between committing a sentence, offering choices and abstaining; bounds the expected wrong-commit rate with conformal risk control; keeps that bound under within-session drift by online re-calibration from user corrections; and records each decision in a hash-chained provenance ledger. We apply the same layer, unchanged, to three device types with public data: MEG typing (19 participants), intracortical speech (one participant) and forearm surface EMG key presses (19 participants). We report effective words per minute (\ewpm{}) of a simulated user against a gaze-typing baseline, with shuffled-input and null-input controls. The layer reduced the wrong-commit rate from 0.610 to 0.059 on intracortical speech with no measurable speed cost, from 0.54--0.96 to at most 0.004 on EMG, and from 0.997 to 0 on MEG, and it committed no text from null input. Conformal risk control brought the mean wrong-commit rate to 0.046--0.048 (target 0.05). Under drift, online re-calibration held it at 0.048--0.049, where offline calibration gave 0.0625--0.0635. On MEG, where the decoder almost never proposed the right sentence, the layer committed nothing, and the system only reached parity with gaze typing (difference $+0.05$ words per minute, 95\% CI over sentences $[-0.04, +0.15]$).
\end{abstract}
