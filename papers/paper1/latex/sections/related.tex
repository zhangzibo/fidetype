\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Brain-to-text decoders.} Non-invasive MEG decoding of typed sentences reached a mean character error rate (CER) of 0.29 with a keystroke-locked (synchronous) model \citep{levy2026brain2qwerty}. A second version decodes sentences from the continuous recording without key times (asynchronous) and adds an LLM stage \citep{zhang2026brain2qwertyv2}. Other non-invasive work decodes perceived speech \citep{defossez2023decoding} or words with large pre-trained models \citep{jayalath2025unlocking,jayalath2026megxl}. Intracortical decoders reach high accuracy for attempted handwriting \citep{willett2021handwriting}, speech \citep{willett2023speech,card2024accurate,metzger2022spelling} and typing \citep{jude2026bimanual}; public benchmarks expose their phoneme logits \citep{willett2024lessons,card2025data}. Surface EMG text entry now exists at scale for the wrist \citep{sivakumar2024emg2qwerty,ctrllabs2025generic} and the forearm \citep{achituve2025emg}. Most of these decoders use connectionist temporal classification (CTC) or a related sequence loss \citep{graves2006ctc} with beam search and an LM \citep{hannun2014deepspeech}. In the intracortical speech study whose data we use, decoded words were displayed on a screen and completed sentences were then read aloud by text-to-speech \citep{card2024accurate}. Our layer acts at this point: it decides whether a decoded sentence is committed (sent or spoken), offered for confirmation, or not shown. \todo{R1: check in the supplement of Card et al.\ whether the participant confirmed each sentence before text-to-speech; describe that interaction if so.}

\paragraph{Error handling in BCI spellers.} Spellers based on P300 or RSVP commit a character when its posterior passes a threshold, and stop collecting evidence early when confidence is high \citep{schreuder2013dynamic,throckmorton2013bayesian}; with users with ALS, dynamic stopping raised communication rates \citep{mainsah2015dynamic}. LM priors are fused with the EEG evidence in these spellers \citep{orhan2012rsvp,oken2014rsvp,speier2016nlp}. Error-related potentials (ErrPs) in the EEG after a wrong selection allow automatic error detection and correction \citep{schalk2000errp,ferrez2008errp,dalseno2010errp,spuler2012errp,chavarriaga2014errare}. They are the BCI field's answer to errors that the user sees but does not correct explicitly, and a possible implicit feedback signal for our online controller. Asynchronous BCIs detect a ``no-control'' state, so that no output is produced when the user does not intend to communicate \citep{mason2000asynchronous,zhang2008asynchronous,aloise2011asynchronous}. In myoelectric pattern recognition, confidence-based rejection withholds uncertain decisions \citep{scheme2013rejection}. These rules are commit/abstain rules with an accuracy target, built into one decoder and one paradigm. Our rule acts on sentence-level LM output, reads only the decoder's per-frame output, and states a finite-sample bound.

\paragraph{Adaptive decoders.} Closed-loop decoder adaptation \citep{orsborn2014clda}, self-calibration during virtual typing \citep{jarosiewicz2015selfcal}, manifold alignment \citep{degenhart2020stabilization} and unsupervised recalibration \citep{fan2023plugandplay,wilson2023recal} keep intracortical decoders accurate over days. These methods change the decoder. Our online controller leaves the decoder fixed and re-tunes only the commit threshold from user feedback; the two approaches can be combined.

\paragraph{Calibration, selective prediction and abstention.} Modern neural networks are over-confident; temperature scaling is a simple and strong fix \citep{guo2017calibration}, but calibration degrades under dataset shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}. The maximum softmax probability is a strong baseline for detecting errors \citep{hendrycks2017baseline}, and selective classification trades coverage for risk \citep{geifman2017selective}. In BCI, confidence-aware decoding with abstention was shown for EEG speech commands on one device \citep{kim2025confidence}, and better calibration and alternatives improved the fusion of brain-to-text output with other inputs \citep{huang2025cocontrol}. Spoken dialogue systems accept or reject a recognised utterance by comparing its confidence with thresholds set from the costs of misunderstandings and false rejections \citep{bohus2005error}. This is a commit/abstain decision under another name, without a finite-sample guarantee.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction and risk control.} Conformal methods give distribution-free, finite-sample guarantees \citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}. Conformal risk control (CRC) bounds the expected value of a monotone loss \citep{angelopoulos2024crc}; Learn-then-Test (LTT) bounds the risk with high probability by multiple testing \citep{angelopoulos2021ltt}. Under distribution shift, weighted and non-exchangeable variants give approximate bounds \citep{tibshirani2019covariate,barber2023beyond,farinhas2024nonexch}, and online methods adapt a threshold from feedback with long-run guarantees for arbitrary sequences \citep{gibbs2021aci,gibbs2022online,angelopoulos2023pid,feldman2023rolling}. Conformal methods were applied to speech recognition \citep{ernez2023conformal}, to the choice of how many hypotheses an LLM corrector of speech recognition output receives, with Learn-then-Test \citep{damri2026risk}, and to LM outputs and abstention \citep{quach2024conformal,mohri2024factuality,abbasiyadkori2024abstention}. We apply CRC, LTT and online risk control to the commit decision of neural text entry, and we test them on three recording modalities.

\paragraph{Hallucination in LM-assisted decoding.} LM stages can produce fluent text that the input does not support. In speech recognition this causes real harm \citep{koenecke2024careless}, and generative error correction can replace correct words \citep{chen2023hyporadise}. Reliability-oriented metrics that reward abstention on uncertain input are emerging for ASR \citep{huang2026ras}.

\paragraph{Text-entry and BCI metrics.} BCI work often reports information transfer rate \citep{wolpaw2002bci} or CER and word error rate (WER). Utility metrics \citep{dalseno2010utility} and the practical bit rate \citep{townsend2010checkerboard} charge a speller for the time needed to correct its errors; text-entry research uses error metrics such as MSD and KSPC \citep{soukoreff2003metrics}. Studies with gaze-typing users quantify the time cost of selections and corrections \citep{cai2024gaze}, and LM-based prediction speeds up gaze typing \citep{ward2000dasher,cai2024gaze}. Our \ewpm{} (\cref{sec:protocol}) is close to the utility metric: it counts correct words per unit of total time, including corrections, and we report it next to an explicit no-BCI baseline.

\paragraph{Interoperability standards.} Apple's BCI HID profile \citep{applebcihid}, W3C EMMA \citep{w3cemma2009}, Lab Streaming Layer \citep{kothe2024lsl}, BIDS \citep{gorgolewski2016bids}, Neurodata Without Borders \citep{rubel2022nwb}, the BCI2000 and OpenViBE platforms \citep{schalk2004bci2000,renard2010openvibe}, IEEE P2731 terminology \citep{ieeep2731} and the IEEE P2794 reporting standard in development \citep{ieeep2794,eiber2021reporting} each cover part of the stack: OS input events, multimodal annotation, signal transport, data at rest, real-time experiment platforms, vocabulary and reporting. Benchmarks such as MOABB and NeuralBench unify decoder evaluation \citep{jayaram2018moabb,banville2026neuralbench}. None of them defines a per-frame decoded-output format with confidence semantics.
