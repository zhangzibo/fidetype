\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Brain-to-text decoders.} MEG decoding of typed sentences reached a CER of 0.29 with a keystroke-locked (synchronous) model \citep{levy2026brain2qwerty}; a second version decodes without key times (asynchronous) and adds an LLM stage \citep{zhang2026brain2qwertyv2}. Other non-invasive work decodes perceived speech or words \citep{defossez2023decoding,jayalath2025unlocking,jayalath2026megxl}. Intracortical decoders reach high accuracy for handwriting, speech and typing \citep{willett2021handwriting,willett2023speech,card2024accurate,metzger2022spelling,jude2026bimanual}, with public phoneme logits \citep{willett2024lessons,card2025data}; sEMG text entry exists for the wrist and forearm \citep{sivakumar2024emg2qwerty,ctrllabs2025generic,achituve2025emg}. Most use CTC \citep{graves2006ctc} with beam search and an LM \citep{hannun2014deepspeech}. In the intracortical study whose data we use, decoded words were displayed while the participant spoke; a sentence was finalised after 6\,s without speech or when he selected an on-screen button by eye gaze, it was then read aloud by text-to-speech, and he rated it afterwards as correct or not; correctly rated sentences fine-tuned the decoder online \citep{card2024accurate}. There was thus no confirmation before text-to-speech in that interface, which corresponds to our \act{commit} (a later interface version let the participant control when text-to-speech played). Other speech neuroprostheses make the same decision implicitly \citep{moses2021neuroprosthesis,metzger2023avatar,wairagkar2025voice}.

\paragraph{Error handling in BCIs.} P300 and RSVP spellers commit a character when its posterior passes a threshold and stop early when confident \citep{schreuder2013dynamic,throckmorton2013bayesian,mainsah2015dynamic}, often with an LM prior \citep{orhan2012rsvp,oken2014rsvp,speier2016nlp}. Error-related potentials allow automatic error detection \citep{schalk2000errp,ferrez2008errp,dalseno2010errp,spuler2012errp,chavarriaga2014errare}; asynchronous BCIs detect a ``no-control'' state \citep{mason2000asynchronous,zhang2008asynchronous,aloise2011asynchronous}; myoelectric control rejects uncertain decisions \citep{scheme2013rejection}; adaptive decoders change the decoder over days \citep{orsborn2014clda,jarosiewicz2015selfcal,degenhart2020stabilization,fan2023plugandplay,wilson2023recal}. These rules are built into one decoder and paradigm; ours reads only the decoder's per-frame output, leaves the decoder fixed, and states a finite-sample bound.

\paragraph{Calibration, selective prediction and risk control.} Temperature scaling fixes over-confidence \citep{guo2017calibration} but degrades under shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}; the maximum softmax probability detects errors \citep{hendrycks2017baseline}; selective classification trades coverage for risk, and selection with guaranteed risk (SGR) bounds the error among accepted items with high probability \citep{geifman2017selective}; risk-controlling prediction sets give the corresponding high-probability bound for set-valued predictions \citep{bates2021rcps}. In BCI, confidence-aware abstention \citep{kim2025confidence} and calibrated alternatives \citep{huang2025cocontrol} were shown for single decoders; dialogue systems accept or reject utterances by cost-based thresholds \citep{bohus2005error}. Conformal risk control (CRC) bounds the expected value of a monotone loss \citep{angelopoulos2021gentle,angelopoulos2024crc}, Learn-then-Test (LTT) bounds the risk with high probability \citep{angelopoulos2021ltt}, weighted variants give approximate bounds under shift \citep{tibshirani2019covariate,barber2023beyond,farinhas2024nonexch}, and online methods give long-run guarantees for arbitrary sequences \citep{gibbs2021aci,gibbs2022online,angelopoulos2023pid,feldman2023rolling}. We use these methods as published; our contribution is their use and evaluation as a commit rule. Conformal methods were applied to speech recognition \citep{ernez2023conformal,damri2026risk} and to LM outputs and abstention \citep{quach2024conformal,mohri2024factuality,abbasiyadkori2024abstention}, where LM stages can produce fluent unsupported text \citep{chen2023hyporadise,huang2026ras}.

\paragraph{Metrics and standards.} Utility metrics \citep{dalseno2010utility}, the practical bit rate \citep{townsend2010checkerboard} and text-entry metrics \citep{soukoreff2003metrics} charge for corrections, unlike information transfer rate \citep{wolpaw2002bci}; LM prediction speeds up gaze typing \citep{ward2000dasher,cai2024gaze}, although scanning prediction lists has a cognitive cost that can offset the saved keystrokes \citep{koester1994wordprediction}. Our \ewpm{} is close to the utility metric. NWB \citep{rubel2022nwb}, BCI2000 and OpenViBE \citep{schalk2004bci2000,renard2010openvibe}, IEEE P2731 and P2794 \citep{ieeep2731,ieeep2794,eiber2021reporting} and decoder benchmarks \citep{jayaram2018moabb,banville2026neuralbench} each cover part of the stack; none defines a per-frame decoded-output format with confidence semantics.
