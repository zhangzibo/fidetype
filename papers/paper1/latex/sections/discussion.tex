\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{A trust layer makes a fixed decoder safer, not faster.} On every device type, the same layer, with only a new adapter, turned frequent wrong commits into rare ones. Where the decoder was good enough for its top sentence to be right often (intracortical speech, session-calibrated sEMG), this came at no speed cost and the system stayed far faster than gaze typing. Where the decoder was weak (MEG, cross-subject sEMG), the layer refused to commit and fell back to offers or typing, so the system stayed at or below gaze typing. The layer cannot create information that the decoder does not have; it can only stop the LM from presenting a guess as an answer. On MEG, our result is therefore a safety result, not a usefulness result.

\paragraph{Strong decoders still need a gate.} The published B2T '25 decoder has a WER of 0.043, yet it committed a wrong sentence for 19\% of trials. Gating its text by agreement with the raw neural evidence kept 96\% of its speed and reduced wrong commits to 4\%. This gate needed only the stream and the text, not the decoder's weights, which is the use case a vendor-neutral format is meant to enable.

\paragraph{Per-session calibration matters more than the gate for speed.} On sEMG, a decoder that saw half a session of the user's data roughly doubled \ewpm{} over one trained on another day (8.02 vs 4.68). The trust layer made both safe; only calibration made one fast. For a product, a short calibration block at the start of each session is worth more than any downstream policy, and the same block can serve as the calibration set for CRC.

\paragraph{How to state the guarantees.} Our results suggest a precise wording. With CRC: ``the expected wrong-commit rate is at most 0.05 for this user in this session, given $n$ calibration sentences, if the session does not drift''; individual sessions can exceed it. With LTT: ``with probability at least 0.9, the wrong-commit rate is at most 0.05''. With online re-calibration: ``the average wrong-commit rate over the $T$ items of this session is at most 0.05 plus a term of order $1/T$, for errors the system observes''. Each statement should come with the measured silent-error rate, because silent errors are outside all of them.

\paragraph{What standardisation buys.} One stream format let us apply one gate, one risk controller and one ledger to three device types with a few hundred lines of adapter code each. The conformance suite turned implicit assumptions into checks and found real bugs in our own producers.
