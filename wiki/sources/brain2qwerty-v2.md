# Brain2Qwerty v2 (Meta preprint, June 2026)

- **Citation**: Zhang M.\*, Lévy J.\*, Rommel C., Rapin J., Bel C., Bonnaire J., Nieto D., Bourdillon P.†, Pinet S.†, d'Ascoli S.†, Moreau T.†, King J.-R.† *Accurate Decoding of Natural Sentences from Non-Invasive Brain Recordings.* Meta preprint, 29 Jun 2026. (\* equal contribution; † joint supervision)
- **Link**: https://facebookresearch.github.io/brain2qwerty/assets/brain2qwerty_v2.pdf (project page: https://facebookresearch.github.io/brain2qwerty/)
- **Raw**: `references/papers/brain2qwerty_v2_meta_preprint.pdf` (+ `.txt`)

## TL;DR
The model decodes full sentences from the **continuous** MEG recording, with no keystroke timings, so the approach is [asynchronous](../concepts/synchronous-vs-asynchronous-decoding.md). The pipeline has three stages:
- a [CTC](../concepts/ctc.md) Conformer encoder,
- a word-level contrastive "CTC tokenizer",
- an LLM (Qwen3-4B + LoRA) that reads both the CTC text and the MEG word tokens. This is the [NeuroLLM](../concepts/neuro-llm.md).

It was trained on **10× more data per subject** than v1. Mean **WER is 39%**, and the best subject reaches 22%. Performance scales log-linearly with data and shows no plateau.

## Protocol
- **Participants**: 9 healthy native English speakers, right-handed proficient typists. 50% women, mean age 34.6.
- **Recording**: **10 sessions × ~1 h each**, 90 sessions in total, plus an anatomical MRI per subject.
- **Trial**: the sentence is played as audio over headphones, followed by a 1.5 s fixation cross. The participant then types it with no visual text feedback (the rotating square is kept from v1).
- **Stimuli**: 2,560 shared sentences, drawn from 20k simple English sentences generated by Llama 4 with no contractions. Including pilot sessions there are **2,724 unique sentences**, and **22,000 typed sentences** in total. Each sentence is typed **once** per subject.
- **Hardware**: the same MEGIN 306-channel MEG and HybridMojo keyboard as v1.
- **Data**: [EnglishBCBL](../entities/englishbcbl-dataset.md), **embargoed** until journal publication.

## Pipeline
- **Preprocessing**: 0.5–45 Hz bandpass, 50 Hz notch filter, downsample to 100 Hz, per-recording RobustScaler, clamp at ±5. No SSP.
- **Segments**: each segment runs from 400 ms before the first keystroke to the end of the sentence, plus 400–500 ms. Training applies random jitter.
- **Augmentation**: per-channel offset ~N(0, 0.3), SpecAugment-style time and channel masking, and time-stretching by 0.8–1.2×.
- **Splits**: 80/10/10, assigned by hashing the sentence text, so there is zero text leakage across subjects.
- **Vocabulary**: 28 classes (CTC blank, a–z, space).

**Encoder ("Encoder Async")**:
- [BrainModule](../concepts/brainmodule.md): Fourier sensor-position features mapped to 270 virtual channels, a per-subject affine layer, then a 4-layer dilated conv (hidden size 1,500).
- A stride-4 convolution brings the frame rate to ~40 ms.
- A 4-layer **Conformer** (d = 1024, 4 heads, kernel 17).
- CTC loss with an auxiliary CTC head: L = 0.3·final + 0.7·aux.

**Aligner**:
- Frames are split wherever greedy CTC emits a space. Each chunk is passed through an MLP and mean-pooled into one MEG word token, then projected into LLM embedding space.
- Training uses a **SigLIP** contrastive loss against the LLM's input word embeddings, with a **hard DTW** to pair predicted and true words.

**[NeuroLLM](../concepts/neuro-llm.md)**:
- Model: Qwen3-4B with LoRA r = 128, α = 256, applied to all linear layers.
- Prompt: `CTC: <text> \nMEG: <tokens> \nOutput:`
- Modality dropout 0.1, label smoothing 0.02, beam search 16.
- [Model soup](../concepts/model-soup.md): one adapter is trained per subject, then the adapters are averaged.

**Training**:
- AdamW (lr 8e-4), BF16.
- End-to-end staged training: CTC for epochs 0–149, then +contrastive for 150–224, then +LLM for 225–274. This takes **19.5 h on 8× A100-80GB**.
- The best results came from freezing the encoder and running a standalone LoRA fine-tune on **one GPU** for 30 epochs.

## Key results (9 subjects, mean ± SEM)
| Decoder | CER | WER | SemER |
|---|---|---|---|
| Encoder (greedy CTC) | 0.28 ± 0.03 | 0.55 ± 0.04 | 0.096 |
| Encoder + 6-gram char LM (WikiText-103) | **0.26 ± 0.03** | 0.43 ± 0.04 | 0.085 |
| **Brain2Qwerty v2** | 0.31 ± 0.03 | **0.39 ± 0.04** | **0.059** |
| v2 with no MEG tokens (LLM reads CTC text only) | 0.38 | 0.49 | 0.067 |

**Sentence-level accuracy**:
- **Best subject**: 22% WER. 28% of sentences are word-perfect and 47% are within one word edit.
- **Median subject**: 15% word-perfect.
- **Worst subject**: 4% word-perfect.

**Sync vs async encoders** (Fig. 1D–E):
| Dataset | Sync CER | Async CER |
|---|---|---|
| SpanishBCBL | 0.39 | 0.59 |
| EnglishBCBL | 0.23 | 0.25 |

With enough data the gap shrinks to 2 points.

**[Data scaling](../concepts/data-scaling.md)** (Fig. 1F): CER is log-linear in recording hours (r = −0.99, slope −0.39 CER per decade), with **no plateau at ~90 h**.

**Diversity matters independently of volume** (Fig. 1G):
- 256 unique sentences typed once: CER 0.45.
- 128 unique sentences × 2 repetitions: CER 0.65.

**Tokenizer comparison** (Fig. 4A):
| Tokenizer | WER |
|---|---|
| CTC tokenizer | 0.39 |
| Sentence-level alignment | 0.46 |
| Patch tokenization | 0.49 |

With the CTC tokenizer, 8 of 9 words are retrieved at rank 1.

**LoRA strategy and LLM size** (Fig. 4D–E):
- A single joint LoRA overfits above r = 2 (best WER 0.43).
- The per-subject model soup improves smoothly up to r = 128.
- The model soup keeps improving with LLM size, from 0.6B to 1.7B to 4B.

**Cross-subject** (Table 1), WER for the best / median / worst subject:
| Training | Best | Median | Worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per-subject from scratch | 0.383 | 0.665 | 0.906 |
| Leave-one-out pretrain + finetune | 0.328 | 0.586 | 0.683 |
| Joint training | 0.226 | 0.478 | 0.614 |

**Sensor ablation** (Table 2), with a single LoRA at r = 2. The full-array baseline is 0.433 WER:
| Sensors kept | WER |
|---|---|
| 25% (76) | 0.547 |
| 50% (153) | 0.490 |
| 75% (230) | 0.467 |

About 150 sensors, roughly an [OPM](../concepts/opm-meg.md) helmet, costs only about 6 points of WER.

**Encoder ablation** (Fig. S5), CER:
| Encoder | CER |
|---|---|
| BrainModule + Conformer | 0.25 |
| BrainModule + Transformer | 0.28 |
| Temporal patch transformer | 0.37 |

Downstream WER correlates with encoder CER (r = 0.78), so **the encoder is the bottleneck**.

**[Auto Research](../concepts/auto-research.md)** (Fig. 5):
- Three Cursor + Claude Opus 4.6 agents each ran 500 jobs and beat Optuna (test WER 0.42 / 0.45 / 0.43 vs 0.493).
- The agents found label smoothing, modality dropout, LLM beam search, a sentence-level CLIP loss and minimal prompts.
- Given an open-ended goal (rebuild v2 starting from the v1 code), the agents **failed**.

## Failure mode
The LLM produces **fluent but wrong** sentences when the encoder signal is weak. For example, it decoded "had she not fallen down the stairs" for the target "cars are not allowed on this road". This raises CER even as WER and SemER improve. The authors note that the decoding objective may need to depend on use (a password vs a dialogue).

## Limitations / future work (authors)
1. The model is **not causal** and outputs a sentence only at its end. The authors suggest a causal Conformer, as in [emg2qwerty](../entities/emg2qwerty.md).
2. **Inter-subject variability** is large (N-gram CER ranges from 17.1% to 41.0%). The authors' stated priority is better encoders through cross-subject transfer or self-supervised pretraining.
3. Participants are healthy and typing overtly. Patients have no key presses, so there are no labels for training or fine-tuning.
4. A cryogenic 306-sensor MEG is required. Possible remedies are [OPM-MEG](../concepts/opm-meg.md) and optimised sensor selection.
5. It is still well behind invasive BCIs: <2% WER for typing (Jude et al. 2026) and <6% CER for handwriting (Willett 2021).
